# GMS CMS content guide

**Status:** Active reference  
**Owner:** Barrels Grenada  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-01

This guide is for the people who write the words around the public weather site
(weather.barrels.gd): desk updates, stories, questions, quizzes and explainers.
The site is run for the Grenada Meteorological Service (GMS). "CMS" means the
content management system where these items are written and published.

The rule that governs everything below: the CMS holds the words, and the
official products hold the numbers. A writer never types a forecast figure or a
warning into a post. The site reads those from the products, so a post cannot
drift away from what GMS has issued.

## What the site shows and where it comes from

| Kind of content | Who writes it | Where the facts come from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official forecasts, warnings, observations | Forecasters, through GMS products | Issued products, never the CMS |
| Desk updates | Forecast desk staff | Written by hand, must not repeat product figures |
| Stories, explainers, reports, quizzes, questions | Communications and climate staff | Written by hand, checked against GMS products |

Product notices are not posted. A product notice is an announcement that a
product has been issued, such as "Evening Forecast Updated". The official
product cards on the site already show that, with the issue time, so a desk
post would repeat it. Do not post product notices in any form.

## Desk posts

A desk post is a short note from the forecast desk about what is happening or
what to expect. It has a hook and a body. The section that lists desk posts on
the site is called **From the Desk** (capital D, as in the CMS help text and the
homepage design). Use that name wherever the section is named.

**Hook (the headline)**

- Under about 60 characters.
- Sentence case: capitalise the first word and proper nouns only, for example
  "Showers this morning, brighter later".
- Optional emoji at the start. Emoji are allowed in desk post hooks only.
- Say the weather in plain words. Do not put a number, a time of issue, or a
  warning level in the hook.

**Body**

- One to three short paragraphs, 60 to 90 words in total.
- Name the places the advice applies to: Grenada, Carriacou, Petite Martinique,
  a parish or a beach. Give practical advice: what to bring, when to plan the
  day, what to watch for.
- Never type forecast figures (temperatures, rainfall totals, wind speeds, times
  of change) or warnings. If the post needs one, link to the official product
  instead, and the site shows the current figure from the product.
- Use metric units only: °C, mm, km/h, m, hPa, km.
- Official content is emoji-free. Emoji belong only in the desk post hook.

**Byline**

- Show the author's display name and role, for example "Forecaster, GMS". Do
  not add a personal email address, phone number, home place, or any other
  personal data to the byline or the body.

**Based on an issued product**

- Paste the issued product's address under Related links and tick Main link.
  The card then says "Based on the <product name, issue time>" and links to
  the live product page. Only the first product link is used; the product's
  figures are never typed into the post.

**Pinning**

- Tick "Pin to the top of From the Desk" in the sidebar to keep a post right
  after the official forecast cards, ahead of newer posts. Several pinned posts
  show newest first. Only homepage editors can pin, because it moves the
  homepage. Untick it when the post is no longer needed: only the newest 3
  pinned posts show at the top, so unpin old posts, and any extra pins sort
  with the other posts by date.

**Social images**

- Every social post needs one picture per platform, or none:
  - Facebook: 1200 x 630 pixels (landscape).
  - Instagram: 1080 x 1080 or 1080 x 1350 pixels (square or portrait).
  - WhatsApp Channel: 1200 x 630 pixels (landscape).
- Fill "Social image (all platforms)" for a picture that works everywhere. A
  platform with its own picture uses that one instead.
- Any platform left empty uses the social image, then the post's cover image.
- The same social image is used as the page's sharing picture when someone
  shares the link. The cover is the fallback.
- The sizes are the ones the owner set (1 Nov 2026). Check them against each
  platform's current help pages before a large campaign; this was not
  re-checked against the platforms' documentation.

## Stories and explainers

- Headline in sentence case, under about 70 characters.
- A kind label that says what the item is (for example "Explainer", "Report",
  "Update"). The kind label sits above the headline.
- A read time in minutes, for example "4 min read".
- Body in short paragraphs. Explain the science in plain words first, then the
  technical term in brackets if it is needed.
- Metric units, as above.

## Images

- Every image has a credit line under it, naming the photographer or source and
  the year, for example "Photo: GMS staff, 2026".
- Write alt text that describes what the picture shows. Do not repeat the
  credit in the alt text.
- Use real meteorological subject matter: satellite and radar images, stations,
  instruments, clouds over Grenada. No generic stock weather pictures.
- Check the image is yours to use, or that GMS has permission, before uploading.

## Quick checks before publishing

1. Does the hook fit on one line in sentence case?
2. Is there any forecast figure or warning typed by hand? If yes, remove it and
   link to the product.
3. Does the byline show a display name and role, and nothing personal?
4. Is every image credited?
5. Are the units metric and the official content emoji-free?
6. Is this a product notice? If yes, do not post it.

## Before and after: current desk titles

These are the desk titles the site has shown, with the agreed replacements.

| Before | After | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Middaay Forecast Updated | Not posted | Product notice (typo as well). The midday product card already shows the update. |
| Early showers, improving later | Early showers, improving later | Already sentence case and under 60 characters. Keep. |
| From the forecast desk: Showers this morning, improving later | Showers this morning, brighter later | Remove the prefix, which repeats the section name. Sentence case, plain words. |
| No Tropical Disturbances Identified in Latest Outlook | All quiet in the tropics for now | Title case removed. Plain words. No product name or figure in the hook. |
| A Fair Evening Ahead, With Showers Expected Over the Next Three Days | Fair evening, showers over the next three days | Shorter, sentence case, under 60 characters. |

When you rewrite an existing title, check its body for forecast figures and
warnings, and move any that you find into a link to the product.
