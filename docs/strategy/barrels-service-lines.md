# Barrels Grenada Service Lines

**Status:** Working strategy  
**Recorded:** 2026-10-17  
**Horizon:** 2026–2035, expressed through readiness gates  
**Owner:** Barrels Grenada  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-17

## Purpose

This document records how Barrels Grenada can grow from a software product
company into a software-led, full-service technology company that competes
across most of Grenada's IT market. It extends the
[Barrels Grenada Product Strategy](barrels-product-strategy.md): products and
the shared platform stay at the core, and service lines are added in stages
around them.

The
[Barrels Portfolio Implementation Plan](../portfolio/barrels-portfolio-implementation-plan.md)
remains authoritative for priority and sequencing. API and integration options
that support these service lines are in
[Integration and API Opportunities](integration-opportunities.md).

This document does not authorize hiring, acquisitions, legal entities,
infrastructure purchases, regulated activity, or client commitments. Each
service line still needs its own discovery, pricing, approval, and verification.

Market facts below come from desk research on 2026-10-17 and should be verified
before external use. A named directory of local businesses is kept outside the
public repository.

## Grenada IT sector map

Grenada's technology sector is spread across business directories, company
websites, regulatory records, and government programmes. No single source is a
complete, current national inventory.

| Category | What it covers | Typical players |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Software and IT consulting | Custom software, web and mobile apps, enterprise systems, data, managed Microsoft 365 | Small local firms and sole practitioners; regional consultancies |
| IT support, hardware, networking | Computer sales and repair, printers, networking, CCTV, custom PCs | Long-established retailers, repair shops, field technicians |
| Telecommunications and connectivity | Mobile, broadband, enterprise connectivity, submarine cable, data centres | Digicel, Flow / Liberty Business, smaller licensed operators (NTRC records) |
| Web, digital marketing, creative | Websites, SEO, hosting, branding, social media | Agencies, freelancers, creative studios |
| Government ICT and digital transformation | Policy, government IT, digital services, cybersecurity, architecture | Division of ICT (Cabinet Office), Digital Transformation Agency and Grenada Enterprise Architecture, NTRC, Grenada CSIRT, CARDTP and DG4R programmes |
| Technology education and skills | Degrees, technical and vocational training, certification, coding | St. George's University, T. A. Marryshow Community College, Grenada National Training Agency, NGO training providers, OECS SKIP |
| Investment and enterprise support | ICT investment promotion, start-up support | Grenada Investment Development Corporation |
| Fintech and payments | Money services, digital banking, merchant payments | Banks, credit unions, licensed money-service businesses (GARFIN register), ECCB regional infrastructure |
| IT-enabled services | Call centres, outsourcing, managed customer operations | Business-process outsourcing operators |
| Institutional IT users | Internal IT departments, operational software, data systems | Inland Revenue, Customs, police, NIS, airports and ports, utilities, NaDMA, Central Statistical Office, hotels, banks, insurers |

Institutional IT users are not IT companies. Their procurement, internal teams,
and operational systems are still an important market for Barrels. National
digital-transformation work on cybersecurity, AI, digital services, skills, and
private-sector digitisation also creates vendor and collaboration opportunities.

## Strategic position

Barrels aims to become Grenada's leading integrated technology provider, then a
competitor across the Eastern Caribbean. Competing with everyone does not mean
winning every contract. It means being a credible choice in most IT categories
and exceptionally strong in a few.

Progress is measured by how many businesses depend on Barrels, how much
recurring profitable revenue it earns, and how reliably it delivers. It is not
measured by how many services appear on the website.

Barrels competes with telecom operators by building the managed services,
enterprise software, customer portals, security, and integrations that
businesses run on those networks, not by becoming another operator. Regulated
telecommunications and payments require licensing and compliance in Grenada.

### Build, partner, acquire

| Approach | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| Build | Software, AI, data, product IP |
| Partner | Telecoms, cloud, payments, specialist security |
| Acquire | IT support, hardware service, specialist firms with customers and reliable technicians |

Barrels aims to own the customer relationship, product quality, and valuable
IP, not every physical asset or specialist capability. It may subcontract
specialist work while keeping responsibility for quality and coordination.

Acquisitions need formal due diligence covering financials, liabilities,
contracts, staff retention, and technology assets. They come after core
recurring revenue exists, not before.

## First service catalogue

| Package | Contents | First customers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barrels Web & Software | Websites, e-commerce, customer portals, business applications, APIs, workflow automation | Retailers, restaurants, hotels, service firms, professional offices |
| Barrels Managed IT | Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace administration, endpoint support, Wi-Fi, backups, device setup, remote help | Start through field-service partners; hire as contracts grow |
| Barrels AI & Data | Document assistants, knowledge search, dashboards, forecasting, process automation | Measurable automation projects, not generic chatbots |
| Barrels Cloud & Security | Deployment, hosting management, email migration, backup strategy, hardening, remediation coordination | Use established cloud providers and qualified specialists first |

The four packages can serve the same customer over time. For example, a hotel
might buy a website, then online booking, then managed IT, then analytics.

The first segment is small and medium Grenadian businesses that need both a
digital presence and practical operational software. This tests several
service lines without entering heavily regulated or capital-intensive markets.

### Product anchor

Recurring revenue comes from products, not only from billable hours. Barrels
Business is the commercial anchor for services work: a small core with optional
modules (customers, invoicing, stock, appointments, staff, sales reporting,
website, messaging). Products still follow the sequence in the
[product strategy](barrels-product-strategy.md#recommended-sequence), with
Events and Tickets first.

## Competitive advantages

1. **One accountable partner.** A customer can get its website, internal
   software, devices, cloud, and support through one relationship.
2. **Local fit.** EC-dollar pricing, regional payment constraints, local
   business processes, intermittent connectivity, and support across Grenada,
   Carriacou, and Petite Martinique.
3. **Reusable products.** Standard functionality is delivered through Barrels
   products, not rebuilt for each customer.
4. **AI-enabled operations.** AI speeds up development, support triage,
   document processing, and analytics, with verification and human oversight.
5. **Deep specialisation.** Meteorology, aviation, geospatial, climate-risk,
   and operational decision support are differentiators beyond generic web
   work.

## Economics and measures

A full-service company becomes fragile when it has many service lines and little
predictable revenue. Track the following per service line and product:

- Monthly recurring revenue and churn
- Gross margin and contribution margin
- Customer acquisition cost
- Support hours per account
- Cash collection

Illustrative only, not a market forecast: 50 business customers at EC$250 per
month give EC$12,500 MRR and EC$150,000 ARR. At an assumed 65% gross margin that
is about EC$97,500 gross profit before overheads, acquisition, churn, and tax.

A subscription product with 100 customers can be worth less than 20
managed-service contracts if it needs heavy support and customization.

## Boundaries kept

- Software built for GAA/GMS is Barrels IP licensed to the client. Client data,
  marks, and owner-marked documents stay the client's. Sharing a monorepo does
  not change that. See the [IP boundary](barrels-ip-boundary.md).
- Customer systems do not share databases. Client-specific work has explicit
  ownership terms.
- The public repository holds no customer credentials, production data, or
  proprietary client assets.
- No regulated telecommunications, payments, or stored-value activity without
  the required licence and legal review.
- A shared platform means shared code and modules, not microservices
  everywhere. Split services only when deployment, security, scale, or
  operations require it.
