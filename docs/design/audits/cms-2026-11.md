# Design audit: GMS content editor, the CMS (2026-11)

**Status:** Phase 1 audit, read only. Nothing was changed in the app; every fix below is a proposal for the owner.  
**Owner:** Barrels Grenada engineering (design mastery programme, issue #1453)  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-23

## Scope and method

The editor is Payload's own admin screen, configured by this repository. The team does not control Payload's look (sidebar, list tables, buttons, date picker, rich text toolbar). It does control what an editor reads and does: collection and field names and labels, the help text under each field, which fields exist and in what order, the review and publishing rules, the preview link, the custom components and the signed-out pages. This audit judges only those.

Surfaces audited, all in `apps/web/cms`:

- the signed-out sign-in page and not-found page (`src/app/(auth)/signin`, `src/app/global-not-found.tsx`, styled by `src/app/globals.css`);
- the six content collections as a forecaster-writer meets them: Live posts, Desk updates (From the Desk), Report write-ups, News, Questions and Discover; the Media library; the "Hide homepage sections" switch; and the publication log;
- the writing flow: start a post, write it, add a picture, add the related product, preview it, send it for review and publish it, with the show-from and take-off times and the social sharing section.

Where the rule for judging comes from: **this app has no lane spec of its own** (`docs/design/` has no `cms.md`; `gmsdash` has none either, see [gmsdash-2026-11.md](gmsdash-2026-11.md)). That is itself a finding (finding 11). The nearest spec is [`docs/design/gms.md`](../gms.md), which covers the public weather site that these posts appear on, and the editor's plain-language guide, `docs/operations/cms-editor-guide.md`.

Method:

1. Read the instructions (`AGENTS.md` at the root and in `apps/web/cms`), the GMS lane spec, the editor guide, and the section "Reviewing or polishing UI: check these before reporting a gap" in `docs/agents/extended-rules.md`.
2. Checked where styles live before reporting anything. `app-feel.css` is deliberately not imported here (the extended rules list `cms` as an exception); `src/app/globals.css` is imported only by the signed-out pages, and says so, so the editor keeps Payload's own styles. A missing hover state, focus ring or spacing inside the editor is therefore Payload's to provide and was not reported. Custom pieces are `src/components/social-attachments.tsx` and `logout-button.tsx` only.
3. Read every collection definition (`src/collections/*.ts`), every shared field (`src/fields/common.ts`, `workflow.ts`, `homepage-placement.ts`, `slug.ts`), `src/payload.config.ts` and the editor guide, then applied web-design-guidelines, fixing-accessibility, ui-polish-pass (state walk), mobile-native, break-ui (worst-case content, argued from the field limits), emil-design-eng, review-animations and the repo's refactoring-ui. The editor has no motion of its own.
4. Tried the running staging editor (`cms.staging.barrels.gd`) with a scripted browser at 390 pixels (touch emulation) and 1280 pixels, following the project's navigation note (wait about 10 seconds, use the dashboard "+" buttons, never retry a deep link). See "What could not be checked" for how far that got. Nothing was created, saved or published.
5. Compared the writing and publishing flow with saved Mobbin flows (section "Inspiration"), for ideas only.

How to read a finding:

- **Rule or reference** is the skill rule or reference behaviour behind it.
- **Size:** S is under half a day, M is about a day, L is several days or needs a decision first.
- **INVISIBLE** means editors would not see a difference in normal use (accessibility, focus, keyboard, behaviour on failure). These can merge on green checks. **VISIBLE** means labels, help text, layout or wording change, so the owner reviews first.

## What is already strong

- **The structure follows the public site.** One collection per homepage section, in the site's top-to-bottom order under one group, **GMS Content**, with a plain name for each (News, Discover, Report write-ups). A writer who knows the weather site can find where a post goes.
- **Help text is written for forecasters, not developers.** Most fields say what they do and where the words show: "Shown on cards and in search. 200 characters at most", "Phrase it the way the public asks it", "Shows as the small label above the headline". Length limits exist and are stated (title 120, summary 200, role 60).
- **Dangerous content is kept out by design.** The desk-update help text says "never type forecast figures or warnings here" and gives the alternative (paste the product, tick Main link). Related links are pasted, not picked, and blank titles and categories are filled in from the live system, with a plain fallback if it is slow.
- **Publishing is guarded in words.** Authors can only submit for review, only the section's publisher publishes, and the errors are written as sentences ("Submit the content for review before publishing", "You do not have permission to publish in this section"). A show-from time and a take-off time are optional and checked ("Take it off after the time it shows from").
- **Previews are signed and safe.** The Preview button opens the real weather site page for a draft through a two hour signed link that is not indexed or cached.
- **Social sharing keeps people in control.** Nothing is posted without "Approve for social", each platform has its own text and picture with the suggested size written beside it, a failure never blocks publishing, and posting twice is prevented.
- **Careful with privacy.** The avatar is a local initials circle, not a third party image. Media needs alt text before it can be saved (required field).
- **Single sign-on page is clear.** One heading, one button 44 pixels tall, an announced error when the link expired, a skip target and a main landmark (`src/app/(auth)/signin/page.tsx`).

## Findings

### Blocking

#### 1. Publishing is a drop-down and a save, with no moment of confirmation

- **Where:** `src/fields/workflow.ts` lines 69 to 88 (`status` select in the sidebar: Draft, Ready for review, Published) and the hook `enforceReview` (lines 12 to 29). Payload's draft and publish buttons are not used (`AGENTS.md` notes that `versions.drafts` is off).
- **What happens:** to publish, a publisher changes the Status select to Published and presses the one Save button. There is no "Publish" button, no summary of what will happen ("This goes live on the weather site now, and to Facebook and Instagram"), and no way to schedule except two optional date fields further down the sidebar. A post that was never reviewed cannot go live in one save: the writer gets the message "Submit the content for review before publishing" and has to save twice. Saving a published post again simply saves it; there is no "Unpublish" word either, only changing the select back.
- **Rule or reference:** web-design-guidelines (destructive and public actions confirm what will happen); refactoring-ui (the primary action should look like the primary action). Substack's flow ends with a "Continue" button that opens a final checklist, and Plane's publish popover shows the public link and an Unpublish button together.
- **Why it matters:** publishing is the one action with public consequences (a weather site and social channels). An editor who is not sure whether "Save" made a post public cannot work with confidence, and one who wanted only to save a draft may publish by accident if the select was left on Published.
- **Suggested fix:** keep the rules but change the controls: a clear status line at the top of the form ("Draft. Not on the site"), a "Send for review" button for writers, and a "Publish" button for publishers that opens a short confirmation showing where the post will appear, the show-from time in Grenada time, and the social channels ticked. Payload supports custom components for this. Size L; needs an owner decision on whether to switch on Payload's drafts, which also gives autosave. VISIBLE.

### Should fix

#### 2. A writer can add a picture only as the cover; the body has no pictures

- **Where:** `src/fields/common.ts` line 58 to 63 (`bodyField`: a plain `richText` with no description) and `src/payload.config.ts` line 28 (`editor: lexicalEditor()` with the default features, which do not include an upload block).
- **What happens:** a post has one cover picture and a caption (200 characters). The body takes text, headings, lists and links only. The post type "Chart" works by making the cover a chart. A forecaster who wants a second chart, a map or a photo inside the story cannot add it, and nothing says that.
- **Rule or reference:** the task the audit was asked to judge ("adds a picture"). Substack and Ghost put image, video and embed in the body toolbar.
- **Why it matters:** a weather explainer without pictures is the exception; the writer will paste a link or ask a developer.
- **Suggested fix:** decide the rule first (one cover only, or the body upload feature). Either add the upload feature to the editor, limited to the Media collection, or write "One cover picture per post; for more pictures, write a second post" in the body field's help text. Size S for the sentence, M for the feature. VISIBLE.

#### 3. Dates and times do not say which time zone they are in

- **Where:** `src/fields/workflow.ts` lines 100 to 123 ("Show on the site from", "Take off the site at"), `src/fields/common.ts` line 321 to 323 (the social schedule, `social.publishAt`, "Leave empty for manual or immediate sharing"), and the live post expiry in `src/collections/live-posts.ts`.
- **What happens:** the help text never says "Grenada time". Payload's date picker shows the time in the editor's own browser zone. The duty note text mentions the 07, 12 and 18 issue times in AST, but the show-from field does not. A person on a laptop set to another zone, or a visiting forecaster, will set the wrong hour without any warning.
- **Rule or reference:** web-design-guidelines (content: dates and times with their zone); Snowflake's scheduling dialog writes the zone next to the time ("UTC-7:00 Pacific Time"). The public site and the dashboard both print "Grenada time" everywhere.
- **Suggested fix:** add "Grenada time (AST, UTC-4)" to the three descriptions and show the chosen time back as a sentence ("Shows on the site from Tuesday 24 Nov, 07:00 Grenada time") next to the field. Size S for the words, M for the echo. VISIBLE.

#### 4. The help text assumes people know the layout of the weather site

- **Where:** `src/collections/desk-updates.ts` lines 15 to 21 (a 330 character paragraph as the collection description), `src/fields/common.ts` descriptions for the cover ("Shown on cards and at the top of the page"), the "Based on" line, `pinned`, and the kinds.
- **What happens:** the collection description is a long paragraph that mixes the purpose with two rules and an explanation of an automatic line. The cover description says where the picture shows but gives no size, shape or file limit (the guide mentions sizes only for social pictures: 1200 by 630, 1080 by 1080). The media field "alt" says "Alt text shown to screen readers" but not how to write one ("describe what the chart shows, in one sentence"), and the Credit field is optional while the guide says every desk card shows a credit.
- **Rule or reference:** refactoring-ui and web-design-guidelines (one idea per sentence; help beside the field, not in a paragraph). Notion and Ghost place a short sentence under each setting.
- **Suggested fix:** one sentence of purpose in the collection description and move the rules to the fields; give the cover an example ("Wide picture, at least 1200 pixels across; it is cropped to a card shape"); write the alt guidance with an example; make Credit required when the post is published. Size S to M. VISIBLE.

#### 5. Preview exists only for some posts and tells nothing when it is missing

- **Where:** `src/collections/editorial.ts` lines 70 to 87 (`preview` only if `isPreviewCollection(slug)` and a `GMS_PREVIEW_URL` is set) and `AGENTS.md` ("questions and live posts are not previewed on the site yet").
- **What happens:** Desk updates, stories and report write-ups can be previewed on the real site; Questions, Discover and Live posts (including the duty forecaster's note, which goes out on a clock) cannot. When the address is not configured the button is simply absent. The preview shows the article body, but the card the post will appear on (the summary, label and picture on the homepage) is not shown.
- **Rule or reference:** the task ("previews and publishes with confidence"). Ghost's and Substack's preview show the post as a reader sees it and as an email.
- **Suggested fix:** a short note in the form ("Preview is not available for this kind of post yet") where the button would be, then extend preview to the remaining kinds, and add the card to the preview page. Size M to L. VISIBLE.

#### 6. The form is long, and the order puts the status last

- **Where:** `src/collections/editorial.ts` lines 108 to 122: fields, then related links, then search and sharing, then the slug, then the workflow fields in the sidebar.
- **What happens:** a desk update has about 20 fields: title, About, Post type, media link, role, pin, summary, cover and caption, body, links, newsletter, social (approval, attachments, a social image, caption, platforms, per platform pictures and text), search and sharing, slug, status, dates. On a phone the sidebar fields come after the whole main column, so the status and the review button are at the very bottom, below the social settings. Payload folds the sidebar under the form at narrow widths.
- **Rule or reference:** ui-polish-pass (every state at phone width); web-design-guidelines (primary action reachable). Substack keeps "Preview" and "Continue" fixed at the top of the editor.
- **Suggested fix:** group the form into tabs (Write, Pictures and links, Sharing, Schedule), keep the status line and the send/publish action at the top, and fold the advanced parts (search, slug, per platform text) away. Fields keep their data; the new groups are layout. Size M to L. VISIBLE.

#### 7. The Related links field asks writers to understand product addresses

- **Where:** `src/fields/common.ts` lines 65 to 120 and `src/lib/editorial-links.ts`.
- **What happens:** a writer must open the product in another tab, copy its web address and paste it, then tick "Main link" on exactly one row, never on an outside address (the error is read after Save). There is no picker for the latest forecast or the active warnings, although the system knows them. The category list ("News source", "Publication", "Article") is shown with the labels, which are not what the public sees.
- **Rule or reference:** the owner decision "related links are pasted, not picked" is recorded; this finding does not reopen it. It asks only that the paste step explain itself. Notion's link field shows the page title after paste.
- **Suggested fix:** after paste, show the found title and category inline ("Found: Marine forecast, issued 24 Nov, 05:00") before Save; keep one example address in the help text. Size M. VISIBLE.

#### 8. The custom "Images and files from the main link" block does not match the editor

- **Where:** `src/components/social-attachments.tsx`.
- **What happens:** it uses fixed inline styles (`opacity: 0.75`, a bold paragraph, 16 pixel gaps) instead of Payload's field label and help text classes, so it will not follow the editor's dark theme or spacing; the 0.75 opacity can drop the help line under the contrast limit in light mode. The download links are plain text links 20 pixels tall in a row, which wrap badly at 390 pixels. The message "Images for forecasts and bulletins are not generated yet. Upload one below." is a status that will date quickly.
- **Rule or reference:** fixing-accessibility (contrast and target size), mobile-native (touch targets).
- **Suggested fix:** use Payload's `field-type`, label and `field-description` classes, give the links a button style with a 44 pixel height, drop the opacity for the theme's muted text colour. Size S. VISIBLE (slightly). The contrast part is INVISIBLE.

### Nice to have

#### 9. The signed-out pages do not look like GMS or Barrels, and the access note is hard to follow

- **Where:** `src/app/(auth)/signin/page.tsx` (seen on staging at 390 and 1280 pixels) and `src/app/global-not-found.tsx`; the stylesheet is `src/app/globals.css`.
- **What happens:** the page shows a deep violet "B" tile that reads as a placeholder (not the Barrels or the GMS logo), the words "GMS content", a headline in a thin system font (the brand font Noto Sans that the GMS apps load is not loaded here), a full width button, and a grey panel: "Use a verified email or an administrator-approved staff account. A system administrator gives access: system administrators have full CMS access, and other accounts need Writer or Publisher access." That is four rules in a sentence, and a new writer does not know which of them is theirs or whom to ask. The words "GMS content" also differ from the editor's own group and tab title ("GMS Content").
- **Rule or reference:** refactoring-ui (hierarchy: one sentence per idea); gms.md (Noto Sans, GMS navy). The test now forbids the old "Sign in with GMS" wording.
- **Suggested fix:** use the real GMS logo (the dashboard app already uses it) and "GMS Content" in one case, load the brand font, and replace the grey panel with two short lines: "You need Writer or Publisher access. Ask the system administrator at GMS." Size S. VISIBLE.

#### 10. Smaller wording and consistency points

- "Ready for review" is the status name, but the guide and the help text say "Save as Ready for review"; a button called **Send for review** would remove a step (see finding 1).
- The sidebar field "Shown as the author" is read only, and the author relationship is hidden; both are shown to the editor with no help for what to do. Hide the read-only fields on a new post. S, VISIBLE.
- Collection names: "News story" (singular) vs the group label "News" vs the homepage heading "Weather news"; "Report write-ups" vs the code name `report-notes`; "Discover" has four types inside. The names should be listed once, in the guide, with the public name beside each. S, VISIBLE.
- The editor guide covers only desk updates. News, Questions, Discover, Live posts and report write-ups have no plain-language guide (`docs/operations/cms-editor-guide.md` is "From the Desk" only). M, documentation.
- Required fields: Payload marks them with an asterisk only; the guide says "the CMS tells you what is missing". After a failed save the error is a toast with a list; a writer on a phone may miss it. Add a short sentence at the top of the form. S, VISIBLE.
- A very long title (the limit is 120 characters) and a title with no spaces will push the list table's first column wider than the page at 390 pixels (break-ui, argued from the field limits; Payload's list does not wrap long unbroken words). INVISIBLE, size S in the list's cell styling if the team chooses to override it.

#### 11. There is no lane spec for this app

- **Where:** `docs/design/` has files for the other apps but none for `cms` or `gmsdash`; `docs/design-system.md` does not list them.
- **Why it matters:** rules like "help text is one sentence, always with an example", "times are always in Grenada time", "names of collections follow the public site" have no home. The extended rules also list `cms` as the one app that does not import the shared touch layer, with no recorded reason.
- **Suggested fix:** a short `docs/design/cms.md`: what the team controls in Payload, the vocabulary list (collection, status and field names), the help text style, the publishing flow, and the reason the touch layer is not imported. Size S. Documentation only.

## Documents that no longer match the app

- `docs/operations/cms-editor-guide.md` describes only the Desk updates collection, although the editor now has six content collections.
- The project note on cms navigation ("find the Sign in with GMS link") describes a sign-in page that has since changed to a "Sign in" button (`src/app/(auth)/signin/page.tsx` and its test). A note for the agent, not the repository.
- `apps/web/cms/AGENTS.md` is one very long paragraph for the architecture section, which is why design facts (names, flow) are hard to find; a short "editor vocabulary" table would help writers and agents alike.

## What could not be checked

- **Most of the running editor.** Only the signed-out sign-in page of the staging editor was seen (at 390 and 1280 pixels). The signed-in editor was judged **from code**: the dashboard, every collection form, the picture picker, the preview button, the review and publish controls, the social section and the list tables were not seen. The test account's access to the CMS (a role granted in GAA Admin Users) was not known, and the single sign-on goes through the Barrels sign-in page, whose human check stopped a scripted password sign-in. The project's notes say to ask the owner to sign in inside the controlled tab; that was out of reach for this audit.
- **Payload's own controls.** The look of the toolbar, the date picker, the list tables and the sidebar belong to Payload and were not judged.
- **A real phone.** The long-form behaviour on a phone (sidebar under the form) is Payload's documented layout, argued from the field list.
- **Screen readers and the keyboard** through the form: reasoned from the field definitions only.
- **Any save, publish, upload or preview.** The audit was read only, so what happens on a failed save, a long title, a pasted warning address or a failed preview link was read from the hooks, not triggered.
- **Production.** Only staging was visited.
- **Motion.** The editor has none of its own.
