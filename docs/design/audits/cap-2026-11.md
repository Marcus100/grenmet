# Design audit: Grenada weather alert hub (2026-11)

**Status:** Phase 1 audit, read only. Nothing was changed in the app; every fix below is a proposal for the owner.  
**Owner:** Barrels Grenada engineering (design mastery programme, issue #1453, section 8)  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-23

## Scope and method

Surfaces audited, all in `apps/web/cap` (staging host `cap.staging.barrels.gd`, port 3010):

- the public pages: active alerts (`/`), the map (`/map`), one alert (`/alerts/<identifier>`), past alerts (`/history`), feeds (`/feeds`), about and terms (`/about`), the not-found and error pages;
- the signed-in warning desk under `/desk` (ADR-0054): board, new warning, GMS alerts, areas, audit, statistics, impact matrix, hazard profiles, import, and the "no access" screens.

The rule for judging: a defect is a gap against the GMS lane spec ([`docs/design/gms.md`](../gms.md)), the warning pattern checklist in [`docs/design-system.md`](../../design-system.md), the app's own notes (`apps/web/cap/AGENTS.md`) or a rule from a design skill, never a difference from another app. **This app has no lane spec of its own**; that is finding 8.

This is a public-safety surface, so each page was also judged on six questions: is the level given by colour, a word and an icon (never colour alone); what does a person see on the first screen during a warning; is old data labelled honestly; is the "No active alerts" state plain; does the map have a list that says the same thing; can it be read outdoors on a phone.

Method:

1. Read the instructions (`AGENTS.md` at the root, in `apps/web/cap` and in `packages/gms`), the GMS lane spec, ADR-0054, and the section "Reviewing or polishing UI: check these before reporting a gap" in `docs/agents/extended-rules.md`.
2. Checked where styles live before reporting anything. `src/app/globals.css` imports the shared layer `packages/ui/src/styles/app-feel.css` (tap flash off, `touch-action: manipulation` on controls, no text selection on buttons, less motion on request), then the GMS palette and brand layer from `packages/gms/src/styles`. Keyboard focus uses the browser's own outline, which was seen working on every link. Nothing in this audit repeats a gap those files already cover.
3. Read every page and component, then applied the rules of these skills: ui-polish-pass, web-design-guidelines (pinned rules), fixing-accessibility, mobile-native, emil-design-eng, break-ui (worst-case data, argued from the code), review-animations and the repository's refactoring-ui.
4. Looked at the running staging app with a scripted browser at 390 pixels (phone, touch) and 1280 pixels (desktop), with the device set to light and to dark: every public page, one past alert, two not-found addresses, the print view, keyboard focus, the three machine feeds, and the desk signed in as the staging test account. Nothing was drafted, saved, submitted, approved, published, cancelled or deleted. The screenshots were viewed during the audit and are not committed.
5. Searched Mobbin for reference screens for the main journeys (see "Inspiration").

Not re-reported here: the items for this app in issue #1688 (date on "Last updated", small links under the status box, the map's empty state, plain history rows) and what pull requests #1694, #1732, #1733, #1738 and #1741 fixed.

How to read a finding:

- **Rule or reference** is the lane spec line, skill rule or reference behaviour behind it.
- **Size:** S is under half a day, M is about a day, L is several days or needs a decision first.
- **INVISIBLE** means the public and staff would not see a difference in normal use (accessibility, focus, keyboard, hidden hit areas, behaviour on failure). These can merge on green checks. **VISIBLE** means layout, colour or wording changes, so the owner reviews before and after screenshots first.

## What is already strong

- **Failure is honest.** An empty feed, a feed served from memory and an unreachable feed are three different screens. "Alert service temporarily unreachable" says outright that it is not an all-clear and what to do instead (radio and TV). A warning past its end time is dropped even from remembered data, so an old warning cannot come back.
- **The calm state is plain.** With nothing in force the page says "No active alerts" in a green box, then one sentence naming the three islands and "at the time shown above". The page is one screen tall on a phone with no scrolling.
- **Every page states its age.** "Last updated 23 Nov 2026, 16:53 (Grenada time)" sits above the content on each page, and a small script warns when a cached page is five minutes old.
- **Level is never colour alone.** Every coloured strip carries the level in words; cards and the map list state severity, urgency and certainty as text.
- **The map has a text twin.** The map is a server-drawn picture with a title for screen readers and a numbered list under it; with nothing in force it still draws the islands and says "No alert area is in effect".
- **The page is light and stable.** No horizontal scroll at 390 pixels on any page, no console errors, no layout jump, pages answered in one to three seconds on staging, and the public pages ship almost no script.
- **Structure is sound.** A skip link, one first-level heading per page, named navigation landmarks, real lists, a print view that drops the chrome, and a visible focus outline on every link.
- **The desk explains itself.** Empty stages are shown, not hidden; "no access" screens name the missing permission and say nothing was changed; publishing, emergency publishing, cancelling and expiring each ask for confirmation in a dialog; a drill status raises a loud notice; tables have captions and header cells; errors are tied to their fields on the raise-to-warning form.

## Findings

### Blocking

#### 1. The public sees a hotter colour than the one the forecaster issued

- **Where:** `apps/web/cap/src/lib/alerts.ts` lines 49 to 57 (`severityLevel`) against `apps/web/cap/src/lib/desk/cap/cap-severity.ts` lines 48 to 70 and the colour list served to the desk (`colours` in the GMS catalogue: Green is Minor, Yellow is Moderate, Orange is Severe, Red is Extreme). The same mapping is mirrored in `apps/web/gms/src/lib/cap-alerts.ts` lines 167 to 175.
- **What happens:** the desk turns the chosen colour into a CAP severity (Yellow becomes Moderate, Orange becomes Severe, Red becomes Extreme). The public pages then turn severity back into a level with a different table: Minor is "Be aware" on yellow, Moderate is "Be prepared" on amber, Severe and Extreme are both "Take action now" on red. So a Yellow warning is shown to the public in amber, an Orange warning is shown in red, and Orange and Red cannot be told apart. Seen on staging: the past "Wind alert" whose CAP file carries `awareness_level` "2; yellow; Moderate" and product "Watch" is shown with an amber "BE PREPARED" strip; a Severe (Orange) one reads "Take action now".
- **Rule or reference:** lane spec, Principles: "Colour is meaning. Hazard hues are regulated." Do's: "State severity in words and colour". The desk's own comment: "so a map fill always matches the issued colour".
- **Why it matters:** the colour is the message. Radio, the desk, partners reading the CAP file and the public page must all say the same colour for the same warning; today the website raises it one step, which trains people to discount it.
- **Suggested fix:** read the issued colour from the alert's `awareness_level` parameter when it is present and colour the strip, the status box and the map from that; fall back to severity only for imported alerts without it; show the product word (Watch, Warning) too. GMS should confirm one table of colour, public wording and severity, recorded in the lane spec. The weather site needs the same change. Size M, plus a GMS decision on the wording for each colour. VISIBLE.

#### 2. A warning that has ended still opens in full warning colour

- **Where:** `apps/web/cap/src/app/(public)/alerts/[id]/page.tsx` lines 78 to 107 with `LevelStrip` in `src/components/alert-card.tsx` lines 51 to 60; the history rows in `src/app/(public)/history/page.tsx` lines 66 to 70.
- **What happens:** on staging the ended "Wind alert" opens with an amber strip reading "! BE PREPARED · HIGH WIND WARNING" and a 34 pixel headline. "This alert is no longer in force." is a small pale-grey box under the headline. The history list calls ended alerts "Take action now (Severe)" in the present tense.
- **Rule or reference:** lane spec, Hazard colours: grey is the "expired" pair; "States every surface must design". Refactoring-ui: the most important fact gets the most weight.
- **Why it matters:** alert links are shared in messaging apps and stay in search results. Someone opening an old link on a phone sees a live-looking amber or red warning first; the correction is the quietest thing on the screen.
- **Suggested fix:** when the alert has ended, draw the strip in the grey pair and lead with "Ended" ("ENDED · was Be prepared · High wind"), keep the reason and the "replaced by" link directly under it, and write history rows in the past tense ("Was: Be prepared"). Size S. VISIBLE.

### Should fix

#### 3. During a warning, "what to do" is not on the first screen, or on the card at all

- **Where:** `src/components/alert-card.tsx` lines 62 to 106 and `src/app/(public)/page.tsx` lines 45 to 86. Argued from code and from the measured layout (no warning was in force on staging).
- **What happens:** on a 390 pixel phone the header is 113 pixels tall and the page title starts at 137, so the title, the "Last updated" line and the status box use about half the first screen. The card then shows the strip, the headline, three CAP terms (Severity, Urgency, Certainty), areas and times, three clamped lines of description, and a link. The instruction ("What to do") appears only after tapping "Full alert and advice".
- **Rule or reference:** lane spec, Voice: "what is happening, what it means, what to do next". Warning pattern checklist: "What to do" is one of the ten parts of a warning.
- **Why it matters:** the page exists for the moment a warning is in force. The advice is the part people act on and it is one tap away, behind three words most people do not use.
- **Suggested fix:** order the card as level strip, headline, areas, "until" time, the first lines of "What to do", then the description; move Severity, Urgency and Certainty to the detail page; when a warning is in force, let the status box replace the page title's space (keep the heading for screen readers). Size M. VISIBLE.

#### 4. An exercise or test message wears the same colours as a real warning

- **Where:** `LevelStrip` in `src/components/alert-card.tsx` lines 51 to 60 and lines 82 to 86; `src/app/(public)/alerts/[id]/page.tsx` lines 108 to 112; history rows carry no mark for it at all (`history/page.tsx` lines 66 to 70).
- **What happens:** a message with status Exercise, System or Test gets the full red or amber strip ("! TAKE ACTION NOW · …"). "EXERCISE message, not a live warning" is a 13 pixel line under the headline. In the history list an exercise looks like a real past warning. Argued from code; none was on staging.
- **Rule or reference:** the desk already tells forecasters "The public site labels this message as a drill and tells readers to take no protective action" (`components/desk/alert-editor.tsx`); the public page does not live up to that. Lane spec: colour is meaning.
- **Suggested fix:** for anything that is not Actual, use the grey pair and put the word first in the strip ("EXERCISE · no action needed"), and mark exercises in history. Size S. VISIBLE.

#### 5. The "out of date" notices borrow the amber warning colour and give only a clock time

- **Where:** `src/components/feed-status.tsx` lines 32 to 42 and `src/components/stale-guard.tsx` lines 36 to 45.
- **What happens:** both notices use the amber hazard pair, the same fill as a "Be prepared" strip, so a website problem looks like a weather warning sitting above the green "No active alerts" box. Both give the time as "16:53" with no date, although remembered data can be six hours old and cross midnight (the fresh line was given its date in #1694; these two were not). "Reload to check for newer information" has no button.
- **Rule or reference:** lane spec: "Hazard hues are regulated (CAP risk scale); brand hues never signal danger", and the same holds in reverse. Lane spec, States: stale ("Latest observation 06:00, older than usual").
- **Suggested fix:** style the notices as a neutral bordered notice with a heavier border and an information icon, not a hazard fill; give the date with the time; add a "Reload" button to the browser-side notice. Size S. VISIBLE.

#### 6. An open page never refreshes itself

- **Where:** `src/components/stale-guard.tsx` (it only warns), every public page.
- **What happens:** a person who leaves the page open during a storm keeps seeing the answer from when they opened it. For the first five minutes nothing says so; after that the page asks them to reload by hand.
- **Rule or reference:** mobile-native, "right in Chrome, wrong on phone": phone browsers keep tabs for days. Lane spec, States: stale.
- **Why it matters:** a new warning or an upgrade can be issued while the page shows "No active alerts".
- **Suggested fix:** let the existing script reload the page when it becomes visible again and every two minutes while it is open (no new script weight, no polling of the API). Needs an owner yes, because the app's notes list the only client scripts allowed on public pages. Size S. INVISIBLE.

#### 7. A missing alert draws the header and the footer twice

- **Where:** `src/app/not-found.tsx` wraps itself in the public layout; when an alert page calls `notFound()` inside the `(public)` group, that layout is already there. Seen on staging at `/alerts/does-not-exist`: two logos, two menus, two footers, a page inside a page.
- **Rule or reference:** fixing-accessibility, semantics (two `main` landmarks, two skip links, repeated navigation). The same file also adds a second set of side padding, so the text is indented 32 pixels on phones.
- **Why it matters:** an old or mistyped alert link is a common way to arrive; the broken page undermines trust in an official source.
- **Suggested fix:** add `src/app/(public)/not-found.tsx` with the content only and keep the root file for addresses outside the group. Size S. VISIBLE (it removes the duplicate).

#### 8. The alert hub has no design lane of its own

- **Where:** `docs/design/` has no `cap.md`; `docs/design/gms.md` lists its lane as `gms`, `auth`, `docs`, `events`.
- **What happens:** the hub quietly departs from the GMS lane in several places with no recorded decision: page titles are plain 34 pixel semibold, not the lane's condensed uppercase `PageHeader`; body text is 13 and 14 pixels where the lane says 16; there is no breadcrumb, no sticky 72 pixel header, no dark mode and no alert status line in the header. The desk uses the neutral shared look (`bg-card`, `text-info`, raw `text-2xl` in 39 places) and carries no GMS mark.
- **Rule or reference:** AGENTS.md, Design: "read the lane spec `docs/design/<lane>.md` before building UI". Extended rules: "Design rules learned in review go into the lane spec".
- **Suggested fix:** write `docs/design/cap.md` (or a section in `gms.md`): which lane rules apply, what is deliberately simpler here and why (page weight, no client script), the level, colour and wording table from finding 1, the states, and the desk's look. Size S for the document. INVISIBLE.

#### 9. The map is hard to read on a phone and too tall on a desktop

- **Where:** `src/components/alert-map.tsx` lines 41 to 107 and `src/lib/map-geometry.ts` (`MAP_WIDTH` 640, home bounds).
- **What happens:** the drawing is scaled to the page, so its text scales with it. At 390 pixels the place names are 9 pixels tall in grey (`#6b7280` on `#e4eef7`, about 4.1 to 1); an area's number would be about 8 pixels. "Petite Martinique" is drawn across Carriacou's outline and "Hillsborough" touches it. Most of the box is empty sea and Grenada takes about a third of the width. At 1280 pixels the map is taller than the screen and the main island is below the fold. Alert areas are filled at 55 percent strength, which makes yellow on pale blue faint in sunlight.
- **Rule or reference:** lane spec: "Legible before beautiful", "Use the `-ink` variant for text under 24px"; web-design-guidelines, content and touch.
- **Suggested fix:** crop tighter to the islands (or draw Carriacou and Petite Martinique as an inset), cap the map's height on wide screens, draw labels and numbers at a fixed readable size in body ink with a white halo, move the Petite Martinique label clear of Carriacou, and give areas a solid dark outline with the number in a white disc. Size M. VISIBLE.

#### 10. Nothing explains what the levels mean

- **Where:** `src/app/(public)/about/page.tsx`. Its description promises "what the alerts mean", but the page has only source, sign-up and terms. No page says what "Be aware", "Be prepared" and "Take action now" ask of a person, or what Watch and Warning mean.
- **Rule or reference:** lane spec, Voice: "plain language first… explain how the weather affects people's lives". The weather site has a legend with guidance lines for each level.
- **Suggested fix:** add a short "What the levels mean" section to About (colour chip, word, one line of what to do), link to it from the calm state on the home page and from each strip. Size S once finding 1 settles the table. VISIBLE.

#### 11. The level icon is the same exclamation mark for every level, and the status box has none

- **Where:** `LevelStrip` in `src/components/alert-card.tsx` line 56 (a typed "! "), the status box in `src/app/(public)/page.tsx` lines 56 to 60.
- **What happens:** the strip's only symbol is a text "!" that is identical for yellow, amber and red, and the green "No active alerts" box has no symbol. The word does the work, which is right, but the third signal is missing for people who scan by shape.
- **Rule or reference:** refactoring-ui: "Pair every colour cue with text, an icon, or a contrast difference"; this audit's brief (colour, word and icon).
- **Suggested fix:** one small inline shape per level (a tick for none, and three distinct shapes for the three levels, a slashed circle for ended), drawn as server-rendered SVG so no script is added. Size S. VISIBLE.

#### 12. Warning text is smaller than the lane's reading size

- **Where:** `src/app/(public)/alerts/[id]/page.tsx` lines 126 and 138 ("What is happening" and "What to do" at `text-body`, 14 pixels); `src/components/alert-card.tsx` (facts and times at 13 pixels, their labels at 11 pixels in muted grey, uppercase); `src/components/site-header.tsx` line 31 (menu at 13 pixels).
- **Rule or reference:** lane spec, Principles: "Legible before beautiful. AA contrast, 16px body, plain language." The pages are read on phones outdoors.
- **Suggested fix:** 16 pixels (`text-body-base`) for the description, the advice, areas and times; 14 for labels in secondary ink, not uppercase 11 in muted ink. Size S. VISIBLE.

#### 13. The header does not say where you are or whether a warning is in force

- **Where:** `src/components/site-header.tsx`.
- **What happens:** on a phone the header wraps to two rows and is 113 pixels tall. No menu item is marked as the current page (no `aria-current`, no visual mark). On Map, History, Feeds and About nothing shows that a warning is in force; the code comment in `src/lib/alerts.ts` line 175 says the status line is "shared by the masthead and the home banner", but the masthead never shows it.
- **Rule or reference:** lane spec, Components, Alert status line: a header pill with exactly one wording on every page. Web-design-guidelines, navigation and state.
- **Suggested fix:** mark the current page (each page can pass its own path, so no script is needed), put the level pill in the header linking to `/`, and shorten the phone header to one row (logo and pill, with the five links as a scrolling row under it). Size M; the pill needs one more feed read on four pages. VISIBLE.

#### 14. Dates leave out the year, and "ended" is the planned end, not the real one

- **Where:** `formatWhen` in `src/lib/alerts.ts` lines 234 to 250, used by cards, the detail page and `history/page.tsx` line 69.
- **What happens:** times read "Wed 30 Sept, 08:46". The history is a permanent record, so next year two entries will read alike. A cancelled alert shows "ended" with its original expiry time, not the time it was cancelled. On cards "Effective" and "Expires" do not say Grenada time (the detail page says it once, in the last line).
- **Rule or reference:** lane spec: "State the facts. Every product shows who issued it, when, for where, and until when."
- **Suggested fix:** include the year whenever it is not the current year and always in history; use the cancellation or expiry time actually recorded; label the pair once as "Grenada time"; prefer "In effect from" and "Until" over "Effective" and "Expires". Size S. VISIBLE.

#### 15. Several links and buttons are smaller than a fingertip

- **Where (measured at 390 pixels):** history headlines 22 pixels tall (`history/page.tsx` line 60) and the "Newer alerts" and "Older alerts" links; on the alert page "CAP XML for this alert" and "All active alerts" 20, "Print this alert" 34 (`print-button.tsx`); the card's "Full alert and advice" link; "Back to active alerts" 24; the footer links 18; the feed links 22; "Try again" on the error page 40. On the desk: 32 pixel inputs and buttons, 28 pixel filter chips, 18 pixel "Open alert" links, a 20 pixel "Log out".
- **Rule or reference:** lane spec, Layout: "Touch targets ≥44px on mobile". The home page links and the menu were fixed in #1732; these were not.
- **Suggested fix:** `min-h-11` with `inline-flex items-center` on the public ones; make the whole history row and the whole card the link target; on the desk use the shared `Button` at its touch size on phones. Because the tap flash is switched off in `app-feel.css`, add a pressed state to the print and "Try again" buttons. Size S. INVISIBLE (hit areas), apart from slightly taller rows.

#### 16. The footer gives no emergency numbers, and getting alerts means leaving the site

- **Where:** `src/components/site-footer.tsx`; `src/app/(public)/about/page.tsx` lines 41 to 51; the fourth link on the home page.
- **What happens:** the footer says "follow instructions from local authorities, radio and TV" with no number. "How to get alerts" goes to another website, and About says "Email and phone notifications from this site are not available yet".
- **Rule or reference:** lane spec, Components, Footer: "'Get official alerts first' (app, WhatsApp, email, alerts feed) beside 'In an emergency' (911, NaDMA, local radio)". The app's notes say the sign-up service already exists in the API and is used by the weather site.
- **Suggested fix:** add the "In an emergency" block (911, NaDMA, radio) to the footer now (size S); then a one-field email sign-up on this site calling the existing subscription service, offered in the calm state and on each alert (size L, needs an owner decision because it adds a form to a script-free site). VISIBLE.

#### 17. The desk's date and time fields follow the computer's clock, and nothing says so

- **Where:** `src/lib/desk/alert-form.ts` lines 144 to 156 and the Timing fields in `src/components/desk/alert-editor.tsx` (three `datetime-local` inputs).
- **What happens:** every other time on the desk and the public site is Grenada time and says so. The three fields that set when a warning starts and ends take the browser's own time zone without a label. On staging they show the placeholder "mm/dd/yyyy" while the rest of the product writes day then month, and the preview beside them shows the raw value.
- **Why it matters:** a forecaster on a laptop set to another zone, or reading the date the other way round, issues a warning that starts or ends at the wrong time.
- **Suggested fix:** label the group "Grenada time", convert as Grenada time whatever the computer is set to, and show the chosen time back in words under each field ("Mon 23 Nov 2026, 14:00"). Size M. VISIBLE.

#### 18. "Public preview" on the desk does not show what the public will see

- **Where:** `src/components/desk/live-preview.tsx` lines 91 to 139.
- **What happens:** the preview is headed "Public preview · weather.gaa.gd" (a name Barrels does not control) and draws a card titled like "ORANGE · HEAT WATCH" in the issued colour. This host's public page would show the same alert as "! TAKE ACTION NOW · …" in red (finding 1), with different fields in a different order. Before a level is chosen the preview reads "UNKNOWN · UNKNOWN".
- **Rule or reference:** the component's own comment: seeing the public alert while editing "catches a wrong severity colour… before it reaches submit". A preview that differs from the real page defeats that.
- **Suggested fix:** render the preview with the public `AlertCard` and the public level table (after finding 1), name the host it previews, and show "Level not chosen yet" in place of "UNKNOWN · UNKNOWN". Size M. VISIBLE.

#### 19. Publishing asks "Publish this alert?" without saying which alert, and greyed buttons do not say why

- **Where:** `src/components/desk/alert-actions.tsx` lines 360 to 378 (buttons) and 450 to 481 (the four confirmations).
- **What happens:** the confirmation dialogs have a title and one general sentence. They do not repeat the headline, colour, areas or end time of what is about to go public. Publish stays greyed until the alert is validated and a box is ticked, with no text saying so. While an action runs the buttons grey out but keep their label and show no progress. The "Confirm cancellation" button is the standard navy, not the destructive style.
- **Rule or reference:** fixing-accessibility, forms: "disabled submit actions must explain why". Lane spec, Button: "Loading keeps the label and adds a spinner". Web-design-guidelines: confirm destructive actions with specifics.
- **Suggested fix:** in each dialog list headline, colour and product, areas, and "in force until"; under the buttons write the next step ("Validate, then tick the box to publish"); show a spinner on the pressed button; use the destructive style for cancel. Size M. VISIBLE.

#### 20. The desk's menu takes a quarter of a phone screen and has no skip link

- **Where:** `src/app/desk/layout.tsx` lines 26 to 62.
- **What happens:** at 390 pixels the header is 224 pixels tall (ten links on three rows, then the name and "Log out"). No link is marked as current. The first Tab stop is "GMS warning desk": the desk has an element with the id `main-content` but no skip link, so a keyboard user passes up to thirteen links on every page. The board's browser title reads "Board | Grenada weather alerts" while every other desk page ends "| Warning desk".
- **Rule or reference:** fixing-accessibility, keyboard access; lane spec, Layout (mobile first, quiet chrome).
- **Suggested fix:** add the shared `SkipLink`; group the links (Warnings: Board, New, GMS alerts; Reference: Matrix, Profiles, Areas; Records: Audit, Statistics; Admin) behind one menu button on phones; mark the current page; fix the board title. Size M. The skip link and title are INVISIBLE; the menu is VISIBLE.

#### 21. A half-written warning can be lost without a word

- **Where:** `src/components/desk/alert-editor.tsx` (no unsaved-changes guard anywhere under `components/desk`).
- **What happens:** the new-warning form is about five phone screens long (4,247 pixels at 390). "Cancel", any menu link, the back button or a reload discards everything typed, with no question asked. On a phone the preview and the readiness list sit under the whole form, so they are not seen while writing.
- **Rule or reference:** web-design-guidelines, forms: warn before navigating away from unsaved changes.
- **Suggested fix:** warn on leaving with unsaved changes; on phones show the readiness list as a compact line near the Save button. Size S. INVISIBLE for the guard, VISIBLE for the phone layout.

### Polish

#### 22. Browser titles and sharing

- The home page title reads "Grenada weather alerts | Grenada weather alerts" (`src/app/(public)/page.tsx` line 21 with the template in `src/app/layout.tsx`). Use an absolute title.
- The title never carries the state. "Take action now: 1 alert | Grenada weather alerts" in the tab, in search results and in a shared link would do real work.
- An alert page has no share action. A plain link to WhatsApp with the headline and address needs no script, and that is how warnings travel in Grenada.
- Size S. VISIBLE.

#### 23. Wording

- "Severity", "Urgency", "Certainty" and values such as "Unknown" and "Expected" are CAP terms (`alert-card.tsx` lines 10 to 17). Say "How bad", "How soon", "How sure", and "Not stated" for Unknown, or move them under a "Technical details" heading.
- "Replaced by urn:grenmet:cap:3f34…" (`alerts/[id]/page.tsx` lines 93 to 104): link the words "a newer alert", not the identifier.
- A history row with no area prints an empty line (`history/page.tsx` line 71); write "Area not specified" as the card does.
- The empty home state says "There are no weather alerts in effect…"; the lane's wording for the same state is "Nothing in effect. Plan as normal."
- On the desk: stage badges print the stored words in capitals ("DRAFT", "PUBLISHED"; `desk-panels.tsx` lines 49 to 52) and the audit list prints "expire"; the area box is labelled "areaDesc"; the "Unknown" severity choice looks disabled when it is the selected one.
- Size S. VISIBLE.

#### 24. Small consistency and hardening

- Headings use `text-heading-lg font-semibold` without the paired line height; the lane says headings are bold and "always pair `text-<step>` with `leading-<step>`".
- `src/app/global-error.tsx` renders with no styles at all. A few inline styles (font, width, padding) would keep the last-resort page readable on a phone.
- `src/app/(public)/error.tsx` and `not-found.tsx` add `px-4` inside a `main` that already has it.
- The desk's preview and readiness list animate with the Motion library but the app has no `MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"`, so the shared less-motion rule (CSS only) does not reach them. The two colour fades are short, so this is minor.
- The desk draws its map with literal colour codes (`cap-map.tsx` lines 33 to 38, `area-picker.tsx` lines 479 to 504); name them in the lane spec when it is written.
- Size S. Mostly INVISIBLE.

## Inspiration

Ideas only. The hub keeps the Met Service look; nothing here should be copied as a style.

- **Seeing a warning and what to do:** The Weather Channel's [severe weather alert sheet](https://mobbin.com/screens/087493d2-1732-4ffe-a35c-af6c3464c230) puts the hazard name and its start and end on one strip, then "Action Recommended" before the description, and ends with "View on Radar". Borrow the order (advice first) and the strip with the validity in it. Do not borrow the raw capital-letter bulletin text or the "1 / 1" pager.
- **Several warnings at once:** (Not Boring) Weather's [alert list](https://mobbin.com/screens/5c5e4b4f-7a37-4650-9e55-01d6761d6b45) is a count ("3 Severe Weather Alerts") over large full-width rows, each one tap target. Borrow the count as the headline and whole-row targets. Do not borrow the missing colour, time and area: our rows must carry all three.
- **The map:** The Weather Channel's [map layers sheet](https://mobbin.com/screens/98764793-2bb7-493d-8e4c-4ff91b9adae4) explains each layer in one plain sentence beside its switch, and Apple Weather's [precipitation map](https://mobbin.com/screens/7463e436-f040-46e2-aac0-c3335f8d0e56) keeps a small labelled legend on the map with a list of places under it. Borrow the legend with words beside each colour and the list under the map. Do not borrow the interactive map: ours stays a server-drawn picture.
- **Warning history:** Better Stack's [previous incidents page](https://mobbin.com/screens/d79fb8b1-ca26-459a-944c-c49768189741) groups by month and says "No incidents reported" for a quiet month, and incident.io's [status calendar](https://mobbin.com/screens/05816d43-88a9-4537-a444-a09ca5456e03) marks days with a ring. Borrow month groups that show quiet months (an honest record, and it follows the owner's rule to show empty sections). The calendar is more than this record needs today.
- **Signing up for alerts:** incident.io's [subscribe to updates dialog](https://mobbin.com/screens/604c8bec-3916-4484-a29f-b49c965cc9e9) offers email and a feed side by side, with one field and one sentence saying exactly what will be sent. Apple Weather's [notifications flow](https://mobbin.com/flows/14b71e00-6f21-41f1-ad79-0825ed0602c5) explains why before it asks for permission, and lets people choose places. Borrow the one-field form, the "what you will receive" sentence and, later, a choice of parish. Do not borrow a pop-up over the alert page or a permission prompt on arrival.

## What could not be checked

- **A warning in force.** Staging had no active alert, no exercise message and no stale or unreachable feed during the audit. The active card, the status box in yellow, amber and red, the map with areas, the exercise notice and both failure states were audited from code; only "No active alerts", past alerts and not-found were seen live. Creating a test warning was outside the read-only rule.
- **A real phone, outdoors.** Sunlight readability, sticky hover and the on-screen keyboard were argued from sizes, contrast figures and stylesheets in an emulated phone. Mobile-native's rule is that these need a real device.
- **Dark mode.** The hub has no dark theme: with the device set to dark every page stayed light, as the lane spec allows (only the weather site has the toggle). Whether a white page at night is acceptable for a warning site is an owner question, not a defect found here.
- **Desk screens that need data or more access.** The board was empty, so the alert detail page, the audit trail panel, the review and publish controls and every confirmation dialog were audited from code only. The test account could open the new-warning form but nothing was typed or saved. Feeds, integrations and settings showed their "no access" screens; areas showed its read-only state; hazard profiles and GMS alerts were empty.
- **The sign-in hand-off** was used only to reach the desk; the sign-in app has its own audit ([auth-2026-11.md](auth-2026-11.md)).
- **Screen readers and the full keyboard route** through the desk editor and the area picker: reasoned from the markup; only Tab order on the home page and the desk header was run.
- **Slow connections and production.** No throttled run was made, and only staging was visited.
- **Motion.** The public pages have none. The desk has two short colour fades and a tick animation; nothing else to review.
- **Mobbin** had few close matches for a national warning page; the references above are the nearest (weather apps and status pages).
