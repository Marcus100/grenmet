Version 1.0.8 carries 75 pull requests since v1.0.7, plus 27 dependency updates. It
has four database migrations: two only add things, and two change index names, one
foreign key and three required columns without deleting data, so the previous
version still runs against the new schema. Production stays on PostgreSQL 17 with
this release; the PostgreSQL 18 work ships the support and the upgrade tool, and
staging made the move first as the rehearsal.

### Staff and attendance

- **Punches from the terminal:** HR uploads the export from the fingerprint and fob
  terminal, and each scan becomes attendance against the person's rostered shift.
  A scan from an unknown device ID, one with no matching shift, or one that is
  refused is held for HR instead of guessed at, and can be decided again once the
  roster or the link to a staff member is fixed. Uploading the same export twice
  records nothing twice. Only the device user ID and the scan time are stored,
  never fingerprint data.
- **Profile photos:** a person uploads, replaces or removes their own photo. It is
  turned upright, cropped square and stored without location or camera data. Only
  people in the same organisation can see it, HR can remove an unsuitable one, and
  uploads are rate-limited. Photos show in the staff portal's header and staff list,
  with initials when there is none.
- **A roster a screen reader can read:** every cell of the duty roster and its month
  arrows now have a name, such as the person, the date and the duty. Before, 692
  buttons on one page were announced only as "button".

### Account and Bookings

- **A separate account app:** sign-in keeps to signing in, and a new account app
  will hold a person's profile, security and sessions. Its first page shows name,
  email and the profile photo. Sign-in to it stays off in production until its
  setup is finished.
- **Time off in Bookings:** each provider in Studio settings has a Time off
  section. Owners and admins add time off on the business's own clock, and the
  section says "No time off planned" instead of hiding when it is empty.
- **Bookings fixes:** the manage link in confirmation emails points at the bookings
  app, and the first studio's service durations match its public booking page. Its
  own website is now a plain static site whose Book buttons open the booking page.

### Weather service

- **Real 404 pages:** an unknown address on the weather site, a missing event page
  and unknown paths on the Barrels homepage now answer "not found" with status 404.
  They used to show a not-found page with status 200, which search engines read as
  a real page.
- **Accessibility:** wide tables can be reached and scrolled by keyboard, the phone
  menu returns focus to its button when it closes, footer contact links are large
  enough to tap, and the LinkedIn button's name matches what it shows. These came
  from a design audit of 13 pages and from checks that now run in a browser against
  staging after every deploy.
- **Calmer alerts page:** with nothing in effect, the page says the all-clear once
  and the green level reads "Nothing in effect. Plan as normal."
- **Media on request:** Weather now opens on the first tab that has content, and no
  video or audio frame loads until the reader chooses one.

### Forecast desk and eRegister

- **Month workbook:** a register month laid out like the paper hourly sheet, one row
  per local hour, with day review, filters and CSV export. Imported archive records
  get their own "historical" state: official as recorded, never sent, still
  reviewable and correctable.
- **Clearer warnings:** each warning appears once per row, in plain words, with the
  reason. Queue buttons are switched off when the action would be refused.
- **Fill from register:** the midday forecast fills its midday temperature from the
  12:00 observation, and says why when it cannot.
- **PDF and feed:** every issued product has a public PDF and an entry in a feed.
- **Checks before a warning is published:** a real alert needs an expiry and, when
  public, an area; coordinates must be real; and a repeat of an alert from the last
  hour gets a warning suggesting an update instead. The public alert map draws the
  islands even when no alert is in force, and the desk's maps draw parishes again.

### Platform

- **PostgreSQL 18 readiness:** development, CI and the restore drill run PostgreSQL
  18, deploys accept 17 or 18, and a manual workflow moves one environment at a
  time. It runs a dry run by default, dumps and restores every database, compares
  row counts, revisions, extensions and roles with the old server, and never
  touches the old data, so going back is a matter of pointing at it again.
- **Steadier deploys:** one slow health probe recovers in seconds instead of half a
  minute, workers start one at a time, new containers are probed every two seconds
  while they start, and migration steps are skipped when nothing they depend on
  changed.
- **Seeing problems sooner:** warnings and errors from the API and workers go to
  Sentry Logs with personal data removed, the daily backup reports that it
  finished, the restore drill runs monthly, and people are told when a weather
  image collector goes quiet.
- **Migrations kept honest:** a test now refuses a migration that would break the
  previous version during a rolling release, and another compares the models with
  the migrated database on every CI run.
- **Less shared editing:** API domains register their own routers, models and
  worker jobs, and the full route list is generated from the OpenAPI document.
- **Security updates:** fixed releases of a dozen indirect dependencies, and the
  regular dependency updates.
